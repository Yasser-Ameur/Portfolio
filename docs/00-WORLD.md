# 00 — THE WORLD

The foundational document. Everything else is subordinate to this.

---

## 1. The correction that produced this design

The first architecture was a side-scrolling path where world-x mapped to time.
That is a horizontal timeline with artwork on it. A visitor holding one key on a
straight line is reading a timeline, however well it is painted.

Rejected. Time does not become distance.

---

## 2. The spatial metaphor

> **The world is one continuous ascent — and the ascent is something you SEE,
> not primarily something you climb.**

**Time becomes altitude. Altitude is delivered as a view.**

> **Revised 2026-08-16 after the first Morocco slice.** The original wording put
> the weight on the gradient itself: walk uphill, feel time pass. Rendered, that
> does not work — in a 3/4 orthographic view, "moving up a slope" and "moving
> away from the camera" produce almost identical motion on screen, so a gentle
> rise is simply invisible. See §21 for the test and the evidence.
>
> The fix is not a steeper hill; that would produce exactly the staircase
> terrain this document forbids. The fix is that **the climb is read from what
> becomes visible because of it** — the sea dropping below the wall line,
> rooftops appearing beneath you, the horizon opening one region at a time.
> Elevation is the cause; the widening view is the effect the visitor actually
> perceives. Terrain still rises, but it earns its meaning at the moments it
> reveals something, not continuously.

He starts at sea level in a walled yard where the horizon is close, and ends on
a ridge where everything he walked through is visible below him. The climb is
literal. Growth is not described; it is elevation you gained by walking.

But an ascent is not a ramp. Real terrain has **plateaus, valleys, tunnels,
bridges, trails, campuses, mountains and viewpoints** — and so does a life.
Growth includes stagnation, looping, enclosure and detour, and the geography has
to be able to say all of that in its own language:

| Life | Terrain |
|---|---|
| steady accumulation | a plateau you cross |
| a period that went nowhere | **a basin you descend into, where paths loop back** |
| a decision that changed things | the way out is up, and it was always there |
| leaving home | a coast you cannot walk past |
| exploration | open valleys, lakes, switchbacks |
| now | a ridge with a view in both directions |

Nothing is labelled. The shape of the ground is the argument.

## 3. What replaces the timeline

| Rejected | Replaced by |
|---|---|
| horizontal / vertical timeline | terrain traversed in x **and** z |
| dots connected by lines | roads, stairs, doorways, trailheads, bridges |
| chapter cards | regions with interiors you walk into |
| scrolling biography | walking |
| map with labelled years | the view from high ground |
| résumé navigation | his desk, his bookshelf, his room |
| "About Me" page | **the protagonist's body** |
| year labels | his height, his face, his clothes |
| progress bar | altitude |
| "THE END" | trails continuing over the ridge |

There is no chapter select and no menu of life periods. There is a world, and
there are places in it.

## 4. The large-scale world

Nine regions, one continuous landmass plus one deliberate break.

### LOWER WORLD — Casablanca, sea level
Warm, dense, **enclosed**. Courtyard walls keep the horizon close. Family home,
childhood room, neighbourhood streets, the football pitch, the basketball hoop,
school nearby. This is the origin and it must feel intimate.

### RISING REGION — the town climbs
Early adolescence. Streets begin to slope. Slightly darker, more enclosed
interiors. School. And the computer — the room where curiosity turns into
making things.

### TRANSITIONAL RIDGE — the first high ground
Graduation sits up here. From the hall's doors you can look back down over the
entire lower world for the first time — **the first "look back", earned by
climbing**. Then the road down to the coast: family, goodbye, departure. The
ridge ends at water. You cannot walk further.

### ── THE CROSSING ──
The only place where the ground genuinely stops. Everything else is walked.

### NEW CONTINENT — Switzerland
You arrive *lower* again — Lausanne sits on a lake — but the surrounding scale
is enormous, and mountains close the horizon at a distance Morocco never had.
Cool, clear, expansive. Lakeside, the climb up to campus, EPFL.

### ENCLOSED BASIN — the fallback
You descend into a bowl. The dorm room and the few streets around it. **The
paths here loop back on themselves**: corridors return you where you started,
the light cycles, nothing accumulates. You cannot see out of the basin.

Honest, not melodramatic. The room is not dark and it is not raining. It is
simply that the ground stops rising.

### OPENING / ASCENT — the rewiring
The way out is not a door someone opens. It is a path *up* that was always on
the basin's edge. Patterns begin appearing in things already seen. Second year.
Low-level systems, functional programming — the world gains a **cross-section**,
and you can see the machinery under the floor you are standing on. Friendships:
other figures walking at your pace.

### EXPANSIVE TERRAIN — hiking
The largest visual change in the experience. Valleys, lakes, forests, snowlines,
switchbacks, trains, small villages. Sometimes alone, sometimes with others.

### HIGHER CURRENT REGION — the projects
Structures you can enter, standing on high ground because they were built late.

### RIDGE / VIEWPOINT — the present
Everything he walked through is visible below. Trails continue up and outward.
Some walkable. Most not, yet.

## 5. Morocco must feel like the origin

The starting region is small on purpose. Walls are close, ceilings are low, the
sky is a narrow band above the courtyard. It contains, and the visitor can reach
all of it:

family home · childhood room · neighbourhood streets · school · football ·
basketball · the computer · games · family life

He begins small in a small world. **The world expands faster than he does** —
that inversion is the whole growth device, and it only works if Morocco is
genuinely intimate first.

---

## 6. His mother

A major emotional anchor, present at three moments:

- **Childhood** — she watches him play. Nearby, doing something ordinary, in
  the warmest light in the scene.
- **Graduation** — she is watching him, proudly. The camera leaves him at the
  peak of his own scene and finds her. She is not clapping. She is just looking.
- **Departure** — she says goodbye as he leaves for Switzerland. The strongest
  scene in the experience.

The emotion comes from **composition, proximity, expression, body language,
lighting, sound and silence**. Never from dialogue, music swells or slow motion.
She never gestures at the camera. A wave would ruin it.

After the departure her physical presence becomes rare. She remains through
photographs, objects, and the deeper memories.

---

## 7. The life, as fact

The authoritative spine. Nothing invented, nothing embellished.

Born in Morocco. Academically at the top throughout school. Played soccer,
basketball, and a lot of video games. **Games are why programming started** — he
wanted to make them. Web development, then a small Unity game, then Roblox
games, then steadily more.

Graduated high school as **valedictorian — top of the entire school**, and among
the strongest students academically in the country.

Left his family in Morocco to study in Switzerland. Arrived at EPFL. Immersed
himself in the work and met many people. During the second semester, League of
Legends consumption hurt his productivity — represented honestly, without
melodrama. He deliberately reduced it and worked on himself.

He began noticing patterns across code, mathematics, systems, people and the
world. Second year became much more intentional: deeper technical interests,
especially low-level and functional programming, and as many skills as he could
take on. More time with computers, and more time with friends.

Fourth semester was his strongest period of growth. More outgoing. Many new
people. Hiking extensively across Switzerland, alone and with friends. Mountains
and nature. Projects he is genuinely proud of. The feeling of finally tapping
into his potential.

**He is still growing.** The final state must not feel like completion.

## 8. The protagonist

He is a body in the world, and he is also the clock.

### A body

physical location on the ground plane · stands on real ground · collision ·
8-way orientation · walk · run · reacts · grows older · changes clothing ·
gains and loses glasses on the real chronology · interacts with objects ·
enters memories · returns from them · **travels between regions on foot**,
except the one crossing.

### The clock

**Age is a property of where he is standing.** Walking from the yard up into the
school corridor ages him, because the corridor *is* a later part of his life.
No year appears on screen. You know time passed because he is taller, his face
has changed, and there are glasses on it that were not there before.

### Not an avatar over a website

No HUD portrait, no name plate, no "you are here" marker. He casts a shadow on
the ground he stands on and is occluded by what is in front of him. If the UI
stopped rendering entirely, nothing about the experience would be lost.

### Canonical assets

Five life stages, supplied and **locked as visual canon**: childhood, early
teen, high school, graduation, EPFL/current. Each has front, 3/4, side, back,
idle, walk, run and six expressions — **65 sprites**, extracted and catalogued in
`public/characters/manifest.json`. These are the source of truth. Do not
regenerate them.

---

## 9. Growth is time passing, not evolution

The age keyframes are canonical endpoints; the transition between them must read
as **the same person growing up**.

No level-up effect. No magical transformation. No RPG flourish.

**Feet stay grounded. The body grows upward.** Clothing changes naturally.
Glasses appear and disappear on the real chronology. Expressions stay available
throughout.

The canon already contains the growth curve — front-view heights run
377 → 394 → 403 → 446 → 537 px across the five eras, with per-sprite ground
baselines recorded so the feet can be pinned while the body rises.

---

## 10. Movement

`idle → walk → accelerate → run`, and back down, with no hard swaps.

Movement must feel exceptionally smooth, and it eventually becomes part of the
emotional language: calm in Morocco, slower in the corridor years, **going
nowhere in the basin**, and faster than the camera expects after the rewiring.

---

## 11. Memory checkpoints

Embedded physically in the world as **meaningful objects**, never as glowing
circles or floating icons:

the old computer · the football · a school desk · the diploma · the suitcase ·
a photograph · the backpack · a project artifact

The visitor approaches. **The protagonist reacts** — expression, a turn of the
head. Then they can enter.

---

## 12. Three kinds of experience

Conceptually distinct, and they should never blur:

| Layer | What it is | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Main world** | the broad autobiography, 2.5D exploration | continuous, walkable |
| **Memory world** | an intimate recollection | closer, quieter, different rules |
| **Project world** | technical exploration | systemic, legible, deep |

A memory may change the camera, lighting, dimensionality, sound or level of
detail. **A memory can be 2D, 2.5D, or deeper 3D when justified.** They must not
all be the same — sameness would make them a UI pattern again.

Project worlds are places, not cards: MiniGoogle as a physical archive, FlowOS
as connected pathways, agent systems as investigation landscapes. The visitor
travels **metaphor → interactive visualisation → real architecture → technical
detail → benchmark → GitHub**. Every technical claim stays
repository-verified.

---

## 13. One authored world, not a collection of levels

Transitions are spatial and natural: paths, roads, doors, bridges, tunnels,
mountain trails, train stations, gates, elevation changes. Streaming happens
invisibly behind them.

The single exception is the crossing, and it is the only one because it is the
only genuine break in his life.

## 15. Decor is not optional

Every environment must feel lived in: furniture, clothing, books, computers,
school objects, sports objects, plants, street details, architecture,
weathering, lights, windows, shadows, small signs of life.

But clutter is a failure too. Everything present must support atmosphere or
story. If an object is there because it was easy to add, it goes.

---

## 16. Scale progression

```
room → home → neighbourhood → school → district → city
     → Switzerland → mountains → huge landscapes → the current horizon
```

This progression *is* part of the story, not a side effect of it.

---

## 17. The ending

A high viewpoint, open. The visitor can look back and see traces of everything
they walked through. The path continues ahead.

There is no "THE END", no completion screen, no final CTA. The world says two
things at once: *this is how far I have come*, and *there is still more to
explore*.

## 18. Technical foundation

Three.js · orthographic camera · 2.5D · textured quads and sprites · real depth ·
parallax. `.claude/skills/world-2-5d` is the rendering authority.
Rationale: `TOOLING_DECISIONS.md`.

**Preserved** from the previous build: clock, store, input, camera, procedural
audio, palette / OKLab colour system. All measured at ~0.8 ms/frame.

**Discarded, deliberately:** the SVG side-scroller world and every scene authored
against it. Do not attempt to preserve them. Do not return to SVG.

### Budgets — measured, never guessed

< 8 ms desktop · < 12 ms mobile · ≤ 120 draw calls · ≤ 24 resident textures ·
≤ 3 live regions · **0 React renders while travelling**

## 19. The first vertical slice

The quality gate. Nothing expands until this is exceptional.

```
Moroccan childhood → neighbourhood → home → childhood room
                   → football / computer / family
                   → one meaningful checkpoint
                   → memory entrance → memory exploration → return
```

The slice also has to answer the question this document cannot: **is the
altitude metaphor brilliant or merely literal?** Morocco is where that gets
tested, because it is where the enclosure has to feel intimate rather than
small, and where the first hint of the climb has to read as promise rather than
as level design.

If the slice says the metaphor is too literal, this document changes. It is a
design, not a commitment.

### Acceptance

Judged from the real rendered browser output, never from source:

Does the world feel beautiful? Does the character belong in it, or look pasted
on? Does depth read correctly? Is the lighting good? Is there enough
environmental detail? Does the camera composition work? Does it feel like
**Morocco** rather than generic game art? Does it feel personal? Does the memory
interaction feel special? **Would someone remember this scene?**

If not, iterate before expanding.

## 20. What this is not

Not a game with a portfolio bolted on. Not a portfolio with a game bolted on.
No score, no combat, no quest log, no dialogue trees.

It should not communicate *"here is my résumé."*
It should communicate *"walk through the person who became the engineer."*

## 21. Validation gate — first Morocco slice (2026-08-16)

The slice was built and driven in a real browser. Findings, recorded because the
metaphor was supposed to be falsifiable.

### What works

- **The character genuinely inhabits the world.** Real position, real ground
  contact, correct collision, and — importantly — **correct depth sorting**: he
  walks behind the yard wall and is properly occluded by it. He does not read as
  an image pasted over a canvas.
- **Sprite integration is sound.** The supplied artwork sits at the right scale
  against 1.75 m walls, feet pinned via the manifest baselines, blob shadow
  beneath. The era registry drives it with no scene-specific code.
- **The engine is not the bottleneck.** 1.62 ms/frame of JS, 28 draw calls,
  37 k triangles, 11 textures — comfortably inside every budget. The 218 ms
  GPU figure is SwiftShader software rasterisation in headless capture, not real
  hardware.
- **Terrain, collision and camera follow all behave.** Walking, turning and
  stopping are stable; the camera lead keeps the path ahead in frame.

### What does not

1. **Altitude is illegible at this camera pitch.** At 38° orthographic, a 2.6 m
   rise over 24 m is invisible — the ground reads as a plan. Climbing and
   walking away from the camera look the same. *This is the central finding.*
2. **It reads as an architectural massing model, not a childhood yard.** The
   camera is too high and too far out; the protagonist is ~7 % of frame height
   when the brief asked for "small but not tiny".
3. **Everything is one hue.** Ground, walls and house all land in the same
   beige band — the exact failure the art-direction skill names first.
4. **The yard is too large.** ~12 × 14 m reads as a car park. A family courtyard
   is half that.
5. **Props are placeholder-grade.** The palm crown is a green splat floating
   above a bare pole; the bougainvillea planes stand edge-on as flat pink
   stripes; the house is a featureless box with no window, door or roof detail;
   the doorway light spill reads as a stray polygon.
6. **No foreground plane.** Same omission that flattened the previous build.

### What I would change

- **Camera** — drop the pitch to ~24°, tighten the frustum to ~7 m, and let the
  character occupy 12–14 % of frame height. A lower angle maps vertical
  displacement to vertical screen motion, which is the only way altitude reads.
- **Geography** — stop spreading the rise evenly. Punctuate it: a retaining
  wall you look *up* at, a flight of three real steps, a terrace whose parapet
  drops below your eyeline as you climb past it. Elevation should be a series of
  moments, not a gradient.
- **Composition** — shrink the yard to ~7 × 8 m; add a foreground plane the
  camera looks past; give the house a door, a shuttered window and a roofline.
- **Colour** — pull the ground cooler and greyer, push the walls warmer and
  lighter, and let the bougainvillea be the only saturated thing in frame.
- **Density** — the yard needs the objects the brief asked for and does not yet
  have: the bicycle, the sandals, plant pots, a washing line low enough to see.

### DECISION

**REVISE THE ALTITUDE METAPHOR** — recorded in §2 above.

Not abandoned: the ascent survives as the world's structure. What changes is
where its meaning is delivered. Altitude is no longer read from the gradient
underfoot — it is read from **what becomes visible because you climbed**. The
sea dropping below the wall line, rooftops appearing beneath you, the horizon
opening a region at a time.

That is closer to what the document already promised in its own ending — the
ridge where you can see everything you walked through — and it means the same
device works at every scale instead of only at the summit.

The next slice iteration tests the revision: lower camera, punctuated
elevation, and one moment where the wall drops away and the sea appears.
