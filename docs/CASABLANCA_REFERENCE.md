# CASABLANCA — Environment Reference

Canonical art direction for the childhood region, transcribed from the supplied
15-panel reference sheet. The sheet itself arrived as a conversation attachment
rather than a file, so **this document is the durable reference** — if the PNG
is added to the repository root it supersedes the transcription, not the intent.

The rule that governs everything here: **urban first.** This is a real
neighbourhood, not "Morocco".

---

## 1. The geography the sheet establishes

The neighbourhood sits **above the coast**, and the street runs **downhill toward
the sea**. The Hassan II Mosque stands at the end of that view, out on the water.

This corrects the previous elevation design. The reveal is not "climb until a
wall drops below your eyeline" — it is **reaching the point where the street
opens and the view runs downhill to the Atlantic**, framed by the buildings on
either side. Far more natural, and it is how the city actually reads.

Elevation still does the work. It is simply that the payoff is a *corridor of
view down a street*, not a summit.

---

## 2. Buildings

Four to six storeys — **much taller than a courtyard wall**, and they enclose the
street on both sides. This is what makes the childhood region intimate: not low
walls, but tall buildings close together and a narrow strip of sky.

Per façade:

- cream / sand / beige plaster, weathered, no two buildings the same tone
- **black iron balcony railings** on every floor — the strongest repeated
  silhouette in the whole environment
- shuttered windows, recessed, with visible frames
- **ground-floor shopfronts**: metal roller shutters, awnings, small signage
- roofline clutter: **satellite dishes, water tanks, small rooftop structures**
- ground-floor detail: potted plants, a house-number plaque, electrical boxes

Entrances are dark wooden double doors with ironwork, a blue tile number
plaque, and potted plants either side.

---

## 3. The street

- **dark asphalt** — this is the single biggest fix for the beige-collapse
  problem. Dark road against cream buildings creates the value hierarchy the
  previous slice had none of.
- raised sidewalks with visible kerbs
- parked cars along both sides: small hatchbacks, muted red / white / silver /
  dark blue
- **utility poles and overhead wires** — highly characteristic, and they give
  the sky something to cross
- streetlights on posts
- street trees and palms, irregularly spaced
- bougainvillea spilling over a wall — magenta, the one saturated accent
- scooters and mopeds, bicycles, plastic chairs outside a shop, a green bin

---

## 4. Football — two distinct places

**Street football (improvised).** An ordinary street between parked cars.
**Rocks mark the goals** — that detail is the whole point and must read
unambiguously. Kids scattered across the asphalt, one ball. Nothing about the
space is built for football; it has simply been borrowed.

**The 5v5 field.** A separate, nearby, purpose-made place: compact green pitch,
**chain-link fencing**, small goals, apartment buildings crowding right up to
its edges, trees along one side. Slightly worn. Neighbourhood-scale — never a
stadium.

The two must feel different. One is improvisation; the other is where the real
games happened.

---

## 5. Basketball

Secondary. A hoop on a pole over worn tarmac, a wall with graffiti, tree shade,
a ball on the ground. It exists; it is not a chapter.

---

## 6. Interiors

**Stairwell** — pale green wainscot below, cream above, terrazzo steps, iron
banister, a window throwing light across the landing.

**Third-floor corridor** — same green wainscot, wooden doors, potted plants, a
window at the far end.

**Apartment** — warm and lived-in, middle-class: patterned rug, sofa, CRT
television on a wooden unit, framed photographs, shelves of small objects,
plants, and a large balcony door letting in golden light.

**Bedroom** — bed with a patterned cover, desk with a computer, chair, shelves
holding books *and* a football, a basketball on the floor, posters, a window of
blue sky, a warm desk lamp.

**The moment** — the boy on the floor with a controller playing a football game
on the CRT; his mother in an armchair behind him, in a pink-red robe, under a
warm lamp. She is not performing. She is just there. This is the emotional
centre of the childhood region and it is entirely ordinary.

---

## 7. Palette — as given

| Role | Colour |
|---|---|
| warm white | façade highlights |
| sand / beige | plaster |
| faded pink | painted façades |
| muted yellow | painted façades |
| sea blue | the Atlantic |
| sky blue | sky |
| sage green | trees, shutters |
| **bougainvillea magenta** | **the one saturated accent** |
| concrete grey | kerbs, sills |
| **asphalt (dark)** | the road — the value anchor |
| dark green | foliage shadow |
| wood brown | doors, shutters |
| iron grey | railings, poles, shutters |

Ground reads **cooler and darker** than the buildings. Buildings vary; no two
adjacent façades share a tone.

---

## 8. Light

Four documented states: morning, midday, late afternoon, golden hour.

The childhood region plays in **late afternoon** — long shadows across the
asphalt, warm light on the west-facing façades, the sea silvering at the end of
the street. It is the hour before you get called inside.

---

## 9. What this rules out

No riad. No villa. No desert. No rural house. No courtyard-with-fountain. No
ornamental zellige everywhere. No tourist framing of the mosque. No empty
pedestrian plaza. No professional pitch.

If a frame could be captioned "beautiful Morocco", it is wrong. It should only
be captionable as **someone's actual street**.
